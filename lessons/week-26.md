# Week 26: Interviews, Offers and What Comes Next

## Goal

Convert interviews into an offer you want, and decide well.

## Before each interview

One hour of preparation per company:

1. **Product**: use it or read about it. Know what it does and for whom.
2. **Their AI**: what features use LLMs? What would be hard about them?
3. **The posting**: for each requirement, have one example ready.
4. **The interviewers**: look at their background on LinkedIn.
5. **Your questions**: three, specific to them.
6. **Logistics**: time zone, link, quiet room, charged laptop, stable
   internet, a backup connection.

Have open during the call: your CV, the posting, your project links and a
blank page for notes.

## During

- Listen to the whole question before answering.
- Clarify, then answer with structure.
- Use your own projects as evidence wherever possible.
- When you do not know: say so, then reason from what you do know.
- In design rounds, bring up evaluation and failure handling yourself.
- Leave time for your questions.

## After

- Within a day, write down every question asked and how you answered. This
  is your best study material.
- Send a short thank-you message with one specific point from the
  conversation.
- Update the tracking sheet.

## Handling a take-home task

1. Read it twice; list the requirements; ask about anything unclear.
2. Time-box it to what they suggested.
3. Make it run with one command. Include a README: how to run, decisions,
   trade-offs, what you would do with more time.
4. Add a few tests and basic error handling.
5. Do not gold-plate. Finished and clear beats ambitious and broken.

## Evaluating an offer

| Factor | Questions to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Work | Will you build AI features end to end, or maintain something else? |
| Learning | Who will you learn from? Is there a senior AI engineer? |
| Scope | Team size, product stage, real users? |
| Pay | Base, bonuses, festival bonuses, increments, payment currency |
| Terms | Permanent or contract, notice period, probation |
| Hours | Working hours, on-site or remote, night shifts (some roles in the report required them) |
| Stability | Funding, revenue, how long the company has existed |
| Growth | What does promotion look like in two years? |

For remote contracts with overseas clients, also check: how and when you
are paid, tax responsibilities, contract length, and whether hours overlap
with your current job.

## Negotiating

- Wait for a written offer before negotiating.
- Thank them, express interest, then ask: "Is there flexibility on the
  base salary? Based on my experience with production agents and the range
  for similar roles, I was expecting X."
- Give a number supported by a reason, not a need.
- If salary is fixed, ask about other terms: review after six months,
  remote days, learning budget, title.
- Be polite and prepared to accept a no. Never bluff about another offer.
- Get the final terms in writing before resigning.

## Leaving your current job well

Give proper notice, document your work, hand over cleanly and stay on good
terms. The industry in Bangladesh is small and references matter.

## If you have no offer yet

This is common and not a verdict. Diagnose from your tracking sheet:

| Pattern | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Few replies to applications | CV and targeting: more tailoring, more referrals, apply to the 1-to-3-year tier |
| Failing the first screen | Project walkthrough and English |
| Failing coding rounds | Week 23 again, daily timed practice |
| Failing design rounds | Weeks 21 and 22 again, more mocks |
| Reaching finals, no offer | Ask for feedback; usually depth in one area or communication |

Keep the weekly rhythm from week 25. Your experience grows every month you
stay employed, which moves more roles within reach.

## After the roadmap: what to learn next

You will be at about two years of experience. To reach the 3-to-5-year roles:

1. **Depth in one area**: evaluation and reliability of agents is in demand
   and few people have it.
2. **Ownership**: lead a feature end to end at work, including the
   evaluation and the rollout.
3. **Scale**: experience with real traffic, cost pressure and incidents.
4. **Mentoring**: senior postings in the report asked for it (35%).
5. **Visibility**: one public post or talk a month; contribute a fix to an
   open-source tool you use.
6. **Stay current**: skim release notes for the tools you use monthly. As
   this folder shows, MCP, Langfuse and LangChain all changed their APIs in
   2026.
